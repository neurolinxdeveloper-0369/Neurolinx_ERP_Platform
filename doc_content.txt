1. Executive Summary
1.1 Vision
The platform is a single, modular, role-based, multi-tenant Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system designed to run three structurally different types of businesses — technology/professional services, electronics manufacturing & assembly, and hospitality (restaurants, cafés, cloud kitchens and hotels) — on one common core (finance, inventory, procurement, sales, HR/attendance, user management, dashboards) with deep, purpose-built modules for each vertical. Rather than forcing a group of companies to run three or four disconnected point solutions (a project-management tool, a manufacturing MRP system, a POS system, and a separate HR/payroll tool), the platform gives ownership and finance one consolidated, real-time view of the entire business, while each operational team still gets a screen and workflow tailored to how they actually work.
1.2 Target Users and Industries
Software / IT services and consulting companies that need project delivery, timesheets, project accounting/revenue recognition, and optionally SaaS/subscription billing.
Electronics manufacturers and assemblers that need BOM/routing-driven production, MRP, shop-floor data capture, quality management, and costing.
Restaurants, cafés, cloud kitchens, banquet operators and hotels that need POS billing, kitchen operations, reservations, and (for hotels) room and folio management.
Business groups that operate more than one of the above under a single legal umbrella or holding structure, and want consolidated financial and HR visibility across all of them.
Primary buyer personas: founders/owners of multi-vertical business groups, group CFOs, and operations leaders who are currently stitching together multiple disconnected SaaS tools and spreadsheets.
1.3 Key Differentiators
Genuinely multi-industry on one data model: a single Item, Party, GL Account, Employee and User model underpins software, manufacturing and hospitality operations — not three separate products bolted together.
Modular and tenant-configurable: each vertical module (Software, Electronics, Hospitality) can be switched on or off per tenant/business unit; a tenant only pays for and sees what it uses.
Role-based, persona-driven UX: over 20 defined roles (Owner, Plant Head, Restaurant Manager, HR Manager, Cashier, Shop-Floor Operator, etc.) each get a purpose-built dashboard and permission set, not a generic admin screen.
End-to-end coverage including HRMS, Attendance, Staff Scheduling and User/Access Management as first-class core modules shared by every vertical, not an afterthought bolted onto one module.
Offline-first design for the parts of the business that cannot depend on connectivity: POS billing, warehouse scanning, and field/biometric attendance capture, with local queuing and sync.
Built for Indian and international compliance from day one: GST/e-invoicing, TDS, PF/ESI, Ind AS 115 / ASC 606 revenue recognition, and multi-currency/multi-entity consolidation.
Group-level consolidation: a holding company can see one P&L, one cash position, and one headcount report across all its software, manufacturing and hospitality units.
This document is the master reference for investors evaluating the opportunity, stakeholders defining scope and priorities, and the development team building the system. It is self-contained and assumes no prior knowledge of the business beyond what is described here.
2. Business Context & Objectives
2.1 Problem Statement
Business groups that operate across software services, manufacturing and hospitality today typically run three (or more) unrelated systems, plus spreadsheets and WhatsApp, to fill the gaps. This produces the following recurring pain points:
Fragmented finance: separate books or separate charts of accounts per vertical make consolidated P&L, cash flow and balance sheet reporting a manual, month-end exercise instead of a real-time view.
Fragmented HR and attendance: each outlet, plant or project team tracks attendance and leave differently (biometric here, paper register there, an app somewhere else), so payroll processing is slow and error-prone and there is no single headcount or cost-of-labour view.
No single source of truth for people: an employee who works across a project and an outlet may exist as different records in different systems, with different login credentials and no unified access control.
Inventory and procurement duplicated: a shared warehouse holding both electronic components and F&B/kitchen supplies has to be tracked in two different tools with no shared vendor master or purchase approval flow.
Delayed, low-trust visibility for owners: the CEO/owner cannot see, on one screen, today's restaurant sales, this month's project margins, and this week's shop-floor output side by side.
Compliance risk: GST, e-invoicing, TDS, PF/ESI and revenue-recognition requirements are handled inconsistently (or manually) across disconnected tools, increasing the risk of penalties and rework.
High total cost of ownership: licensing, integration and support costs for 4-6 disconnected SaaS tools, plus the hidden cost of reconciling them, exceed the cost of one well-built unified platform at scale.
2.2 Strategic Objectives
Unified data: one tenant, one chart of accounts, one item/party/employee master, shared across every vertical module, with per-business-unit segmentation where required.
Real-time visibility: role-based dashboards refresh from live transactional data (POS sale, work order completion, timesheet entry) rather than nightly batch jobs, so decisions can be made same-day.
Cost reduction: replace 4-6 point solutions with modules of a single platform, cutting license sprawl, integration maintenance, and duplicate data entry.
Scalability: support a group's growth from a handful of outlets/projects/plants to dozens, and from single-entity to multi-entity/multi-currency, without a platform migration.
Compliance by design: GST/e-invoicing, TDS, PF/ESI, and Ind AS 115/ASC 606 revenue recognition are built into the core finance and payroll engines, not bolted on.
Single source of truth for employees and users: one Employee record drives HR, attendance, payroll, staff rostering and system login/permissions, eliminating duplicate identity management.

5. Modular Architecture Overview
5.1 High-Level Architecture (Description)
The platform is delivered as a modular backend (deployable as a modular monolith initially, with a clear path to domain microservices) exposing REST/GraphQL APIs, consumed by three client surfaces:
Web (React + TypeScript): the primary surface for admin, finance, operations, project management, HR, procurement and reporting — used by managers and back-office staff on desktop/laptop browsers.
Windows desktop (Electron wrapping React, or React Native for Windows): used for back-office workstations and, most importantly, POS terminals in restaurants/cafés/hotels where a dedicated, always-on till application is preferred.
Android (phone & tablet, React Native): used for field/operational roles — warehouse scanning, HR self-service and attendance, shop-floor and QC data capture, housekeeping updates, and a lightweight offline-capable POS for smaller outlets.
All three clients talk to the same backend API layer. The backend is organized into domain modules — Core (tenant, security, finance, inventory core, HR/attendance/user management), Software, Electronics, and Hospitality — each owning its own data and business logic, but sharing the Core services for identity, finance postings, inventory ledger and reporting. An event bus (Kafka/RabbitMQ) carries domain events (order_created, invoice_posted, stock_moved, work_order_completed, attendance_marked, leave_approved, and similar) so that modules react to each other without being tightly coupled — for example, a POS sale reduces stock and posts to finance by publishing events that the Inventory and Finance services consume, rather than the POS service writing directly into their tables.
PostgreSQL (or MySQL) is the primary datastore, with Redis used for caching and session/token data. Flyway or Liquibase manages schema migrations across environments. Offline-first clients (POS, warehouse, field attendance) maintain a local embedded database and a sync queue that reconciles with the server when connectivity returns, with conflict resolution rules defined per entity (e.g., last-write-wins for draft POS orders, server-authoritative for stock levels).

5.3 Module Enablement per Tenant
Every tenant (a company or business group) is provisioned with the Core ERP modules always enabled. Each vertical module — Software, Electronics, Hospitality — is a toggle at the tenant or business-unit level, controlled by System Admin in the Multi-Tenant Administration module. A single tenant can enable more than one vertical module simultaneously (e.g., a group running both a software services arm and a restaurant chain), and each business unit within that tenant can be scoped to see only the data and modules relevant to it, while group-level roles (Owner/CEO, Group Finance) see consolidated data across all enabled modules and business units.

6.1 Core ERP
6.1.1 Finance & Accounting
Purpose & scope: single financial backbone for all verticals and business units — general ledger, payables, receivables, fixed assets, taxation, multi-currency/multi-entity, and statutory/management reporting.

Accounts Payable (AP): vendor bills, three-way match against PO/GRN, payment runs, ageing.
Accounts Receivable (AR): customer invoices (from Software, Electronics, Hospitality modules), receipts, ageing, dunning.
Fixed Assets: asset register, depreciation schedules (multiple methods), disposal/transfer.
Tax engine: GST (CGST/SGST/IGST), TDS, VAT (where applicable) — configurable by jurisdiction, e-invoicing generation/validation.
Multi-currency: transaction and functional currency, realized/unrealized gain-loss, period-end revaluation.
Multi-entity & consolidation: separate books per legal entity, inter-company elimination, consolidated financial statements.
Bank reconciliation: bank feed/statement import, auto-matching, manual reconciliation.
Budgeting: budget entry by account/cost-center/project/outlet, budget vs actual reporting.
Financial reports: P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, Trial Balance, GL detail, cost-center/segment reporting.

8.2 Availability & Reliability
Target uptime of 99.5%+ for the core backend/API layer in production, excluding scheduled maintenance windows.
Automated daily backups with point-in-time recovery; documented disaster-recovery runbook with a defined RPO/RTO (e.g., RPO ≤ 1 hour, RTO ≤ 4 hours for Phase 1, tightened in later phases).
Graceful degradation: POS, warehouse scanning and attendance apps must remain operable offline and sync automatically on reconnect.
8.3 Security
Authentication via email/password, OTP, and SSO/OAuth2; mandatory MFA for finance, HR-payroll, and system-admin roles.
Role-based access control (RBAC) enforced at the API layer, not just the UI, with data-level scoping by company/outlet/warehouse/project.
Audit trail for all critical transactions: finance postings, inventory adjustments, HR/payroll changes, POS voids/refunds/discounts, and permission changes.
Encryption in transit (TLS 1.2+) and at rest for all databases and backups.
Session management with configurable timeout; optional device trust and IP allow-listing for sensitive roles.
8.4 Scalability
Architecture (modular monolith with clear domain boundaries, moving to microservices where load requires) must scale horizontally per domain module.
Designed to support growth from a handful of outlets/projects/plants to dozens per tenant, and from hundreds to tens of thousands of daily transactions (POS orders, attendance punches, stock movements) without redesign.
Multi-tenant data isolation must scale to many tenants without cross-tenant performance degradation.
8.5 Usability
Mobile-first design for roles that are primarily on the move or on the floor: attendance/ESS, warehouse scanning, POS on tablets, housekeeping.
Multi-language support for POS and ESS screens at minimum, given the diversity of frontline staff.
Accessibility: keyboard navigation and adequate color contrast on web screens as a baseline (WCAG 2.1 AA as a target, phased in).
8.6 Maintainability
Consistent coding standards and linting enforced via CI for both backend (Java/Spring) and frontend (TypeScript/React) codebases.
API-first design with versioned REST/GraphQL contracts and generated API documentation.
Database schema changes managed exclusively through Flyway/Liquibase migrations, never manual DDL in production.

10.2 Key Relationships
User ↔ Employee: one-to-one for the vast majority of users (an employee has exactly one login); the model also permits one-to-many in edge cases (e.g., a shared kiosk login for training) and zero employees for non-employee external users.
Employee ↔ Attendance, LeaveApplication, PayrollRecord: one-to-many — an employee accumulates many attendance records, leave applications, and payroll records over time.
Employee ↔ StaffRoster / Location Assignment: one-to-many with history — an employee's outlet/project/warehouse assignment can change over time and past assignments are retained for reporting.
Role ↔ User: many-to-many (a user can hold more than one role); Role ↔ Permission: many-to-many; Role/User ↔ Data Scope: a scoping record limits a role/user combination to specific companies/outlets/warehouses/projects.
Item ↔ Warehouse/Bin ↔ StockLedgerEntry: an item's stock position at any point is the aggregate of its ledger entries per warehouse/bin, never a directly-edited balance field.
BOM ↔ Item: a BOM belongs to a parent Item and references child Items (BOMItem), supporting multi-level nesting (a BOMItem's item can itself have a BOM).
WorkOrder ↔ BOM ↔ Routing: a work order is created against a specific BOM revision and Routing, both of which are versioned via ECO for BOM.
Reservation/Table ↔ KOT ↔ Folio: orders raised against a table or room generate KOTs and post transactions to the relevant bill or guest folio.
Party (Customer/Vendor) is shared across Software (clients), Electronics (customers/vendors) and Hospitality (corporate/event clients) so a single organization is not duplicated as separate records per vertical.

11.2 Data-Level Security
Every role is combined with a data scope: company/entity, outlet, warehouse, and/or project. A Restaurant Manager assigned to Outlet A cannot view or act on Outlet B's data unless explicitly granted multi-outlet scope.
Group-level roles (Owner, Group Finance) have scope across all business units by default; operational roles default to a single-location scope that must be explicitly widened.
11.3 Audit Requirements
Finance: all journal entries, payment approvals, and chart-of-accounts changes are logged with user, timestamp, and before/after values.
Inventory: all stock adjustments and transfers are logged with reason code and approver.
HR/Payroll: all changes to salary structure, employee master compensation fields, and payroll runs are logged; payslip access is logged for privacy compliance.
POS: all voids, refunds, discounts above a threshold, and bill merges/splits are logged with the authorizing user.
Access: all login attempts (success/failure), permission changes, and role assignments are logged.
11.4 Password, MFA, Session Policies
Minimum password complexity and rotation policy configurable per tenant; breached-password checks recommended.
MFA mandatory for FINANCE_HEAD, HR_MANAGER, OWNER, and SYSTEM_ADMIN roles; optional but encouraged for others.
Configurable session timeout per role sensitivity (e.g., shorter timeout for finance/admin sessions than for a kitchen display).
Optional device trust and IP allow-listing for back-office/finance access, particularly for remote or high-privilege roles.

13. Risks & Mitigation
Risk
Mitigation
Implementation complexity across three very different verticals
Build the shared Core first and validate it against one vertical end-to-end before adding the next; maintain a strict shared-data-model discipline to avoid vertical-specific forks.
Data migration from existing systems (HR, finance, POS, projects)
Phase migration per vertical/business unit; provide import templates and a validation/reconciliation step before cutover; run parallel for a defined period where feasible.
User adoption, especially for attendance, POS, and shop-floor roles
Pilot with a small, motivated group first; keep frontline UI minimal-click; provide in-app guidance and on-site training during rollout.
Offline sync issues for POS/warehouse/field attendance
Design conflict-resolution rules explicitly per entity up front; extensive offline/online transition testing; clear in-app sync-status indicators for users.
Performance under load (month-end, payroll, high-volume POS)
Load-test against realistic peak scenarios before each phase's go-live; design payroll and month-end jobs to run asynchronously/in background with progress visibility.
Scope creep across three verticals diluting Phase 1 focus
Enforce the explicit out-of-scope list (Section 3.2) and change-control process for anything proposed to be added to Phase 1.
Compliance risk (GST/e-invoicing/PF/ESI changes)
Design the tax and statutory-compliance engine as configuration-driven, not hard-coded, so regulatory changes don't require a full release cycle.

15.3 Example User Journeys
"Hire to Retire" for an employee: candidate onboarded as Employee → user account and role provisioned → assigned to a roster → attendance captured over the employment period → leave accrued and applied for → monthly payroll processed → eventually, separation recorded and system access revoked, with final settlement processed.
"Shift Roster → Attendance → Payroll": manager publishes next week's roster for an outlet → employees clock in/out against their rostered shifts → any missed punches are regularised → attendance is locked at period end → payroll run automatically applies LOP/overtime based on locked attendance → payslips are generated and published to ESS.
"Guest reserves table → arrives → orders via QR → pays → loyalty points updated": guest books a table online with a small deposit → arrives and is checked in against the reservation → scans the table QR code and orders directly from their phone → kitchen receives the KOT and prepares the order → guest requests the bill via the QR menu → cashier finalizes payment across cash/card/UPI → loyalty points are credited to the guest's profile automatically.
"Sales order → work order → production → QC → delivery → invoice": a confirmed sales order for a finished electronic assembly triggers a work order against the current BOM/routing revision → shop floor executes operations, logging quantities per station → in-process and final QC checkpoints are passed (or an NCR raised and dispositioned) → finished goods are received into the warehouse → the order is delivered and invoiced, with standard-vs-actual cost variance computed against the work order.
